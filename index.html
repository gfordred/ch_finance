Professional note

What she should understand

Her Emirates salary may qualify for the foreign employment income exemption while she meets the full-days-outside-SA rules, but SA-source investment income remains important and may still be taxable depending on its exact nature.

This page is an education tool for better conversations with a tax practitioner and bond originator. It intentionally teaches thresholds, caps, marginal tax and affordability rather than pretending tax is one magic number.

Foreign employment income exemption / 183-day rule

For SA tax residents earning qualifying foreign employment remuneration: if you are outside SA for more than 183 full days in any 12-month period, including a continuous period of more than 60 full days, the first R1.25m may be exempt. This is a powerful lesson, not a substitute for tax advice.

Marginal tax rate meter

Academic but simple

What marginal tax rate means

Important: if your marginal rate is 31%, SARS does not take 31% of your whole salary. Only the slice of income inside that bracket is taxed at 31%.

🍕

Pizza-slice idea

Your income is sliced into brackets. Each slice has its own rate. Add the tax on all slices, then subtract rebates.

🚪

Next-rand idea

Marginal rate answers: “What tax do I pay on the next R1 of taxable income?”

🧾

PAYE idea

PAYE is tax collected from salary during the year. Filing reconciles the estimate to your actual position.

Worked mini-example

If taxable income rises by R10,000 while Carmen is in a 31% marginal bracket, the extra tax is about R3,100. Her whole income is not repriced; only the extra slice is taxed at 31%.

Extra tax ≈ extra taxable income × marginal rate

Academic explanations

Three formulas Carmen should really understand

These are deliberately simple but academically correct mental models. They help Carmen avoid the two big mistakes young professionals make: confusing marginal tax with average tax, and judging a home loan only by the monthly instalment.

1

Taxable income bridge

Start with income, remove allowed exemptions/deductions, add taxable interest and gains, then apply the SARS tax table and rebates.

Taxable income = income + taxable interest − deductions − exemptions
2

CGT bridge

Capital gains tax is not tax on the full sale proceeds. It starts with profit, applies the annual exclusion, and includes only 40% for individuals.

Taxable CGT inclusion = max(gain − R50,000, 0) × 40%
3

Bond cost bridge

A bond’s true cost is the sum of all future interest. Extra payments reduce principal, which reduces future interest months.

Monthly interest = opening balance × annual rate ÷ 12

Worked tax example

Example: Emirates salary and SA investment income

Assume R90k/month Emirates salary and R40k/month SA investment income. Annual gross income is about R1.56m. If the R1.08m foreign employment salary qualifies, up to R1.08m can be removed before the tax table, leaving the SA income and other taxable items to analyse.

R90,000 × 12 = R1,080,000 foreign employment income
R40,000 × 12 = R480,000 SA investment income
Potential foreign exemption = min(R1,080,000, R1,250,000)

Important: the app does not decide whether the medical-rep-company amount is salary, dividend, interest, loan repayment, director fee or business income. Carmen should classify that income carefully because tax treatment changes.

Worked bond example

Example: why extra payments help

On a R2.25m bond, the first month’s interest at 11.15% is roughly R20.9k before capital reduction. This is why early payments feel slow. An extra payment reduces the balance immediately, so every future month starts from a smaller balance.

First-month interest ≈ R2,250,000 × 11.15% ÷ 12 ≈ R20,906
Capital repaid = monthly payment − monthly interest
Next balance = old balance − capital repaid − extra payment

Rule of thumb: do not only ask “Can I afford this month?” Ask “Can I afford this after a 2% rate shock, while still saving and keeping emergency cash?”
